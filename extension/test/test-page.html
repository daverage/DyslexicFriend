Anchor Contrast Playground

Dark on Light

The classic dark-text-on-light-background pairing consistently scores highest for reading speed in eye-tracking studies.

Designers often add a faint border or drop shadow to help separate content blocks without sacrificing clarity.

Light on Dark

Light text on a deep background works well for immersive dashboards when the surrounding room is dim or the viewer is fatigued.

A lower absolute luminance reduces glare, but designers must check contrast to avoid washed-out details.

Mid-tone on Mid-tone

Mid-tone combinations are tricky; they rely on texture and font weight to remain legible.

Adding a subtle outline or alternating background stripes can make these palettes more accessible.

Gradient Background

Gradient surfaces need deliberate anchor colours; contrasting hues in focal zones keep text visible as colours shift.
